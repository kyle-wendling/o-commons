About Us

The O Commons: Immigrant Artists Mutual Aid is a community-driven initiative that responds to the growing precarity of working as an artist on an O-1 visa—one of the main U.S. work visas for individuals with extraordinary ability in the arts. As fewer cultural institutions are willing or able to sponsor O-1 visa holders and the cost and complexity of maintaining legal status continue to rise, immigrant artists increasingly find themselves excluded from employment opportunities, forced to navigate an opaque immigration system, and left without access to the practical knowledge needed to sustain their careers.
 
In today's gig-based cultural economy, many artists rely on multiple jobs, freelance work, and institutional affiliations to make a living. O-1 visa holders, however, are often restricted to an exceptionally narrow field of authorized employment. Combined with the high financial cost and administrative burden of maintaining legal status, this creates a system that privileges artists with access to family wealth, institutional support, or other economic safety nets while placing equally accomplished artists without such resources at a significant disadvantage.
 
In recent years, these challenges have intensified. Obtaining an O-1 visa has always been expensive and time-consuming, but it has become increasingly difficult to find organizations willing to sponsor or employ O-1 artists.
 
These barriers particularly affect three groups:
artists who have built their lives and careers in the United States and must repeatedly renew their immigration status;
graduating international students who wish to remain in the artistic communities they have developed during their studies; and
newly arriving artists invited to work in the United States who require legal authorization before they can begin their professional engagements.
 
Although immigration attorneys provide essential legal expertise, much of the immigration process depends on practical knowledge that is rarely shared publicly: understanding timelines, preparing recommendation letters, identifying supportive institutions, and learning from the experiences of others who have successfully navigated the process.
 
The O Network seeks to fill this gap by creating an infrastructure of mutual aid through peer-to-peer knowledge sharing and a publicly accessible digital resource.

 Long-Term Vision
 
Beyond launching a mutual aid network, this gathering will lay the foundation for an ongoing peer-to-peer Mutual Aid Network, a Resource Hub, and a Curatorial-Research Repository.

 1. Peer-to-Peer Mutual Aid Network
 
The network will connect immigrant artists who have successfully completed the O-1 or employment-based permanent residency (EB) process with artists currently navigating it.
 
Volunteer mentors will share their experiences, offer practical guidance, and help applicants better prepare for consultations with immigration attorneys. While these conversations will never replace legal advice, they can help petitioners ask more informed questions, organize stronger applications, and ultimately reduce both legal costs and unnecessary uncertainty.
 
Participants who volunteer as mentors will receive basic orientation to ensure that shared information remains responsible, experience-based, and clearly distinguished from legal counsel.
 
2. Resource Hub
Alongside the network, the project will develop an online archive containing community-generated resources, including:
 
a directory of immigration attorneys recommended by artists, accompanied by brief descriptions of community experiences;
information about organizations, cultural institutions, and educational spaces that successfully hire or sponsor O-1 visa holders;
anonymized examples of successful petition materials, including recommendation letters and supporting documents;
practical information about application timelines, costs, Requests for Evidence (RFEs), and strategies for preparing petitions; and
a directory of artists willing to speak with others beginning the process.
 
One of the archive's most valuable resources will be a collection of anonymized sample documents. O-1 applicants frequently assemble hundreds of pages of evidence and often draft recommendation letters themselves—not because recommenders are unwilling to help, but because applicants understand the petition's specific requirements. By removing all identifying information and making selected examples available, the archive will provide practical models that can significantly reduce the burden on future applicants.
 
Together, these resources aim to make an opaque and isolating immigration process more transparent, collaborative, and equitable.

3. Curatorial-Research Repository
Drawing inspiration from artists who have transformed their O-1 and employment-based residency applications into artworks, performances, and installations, I hope to begin assembling a collective archive of document-based artistic responses to migration.
Over time, this archive could develop into exhibitions, publications, visual essays, or performances that expose the extraordinary labor, emotional strain, financial burden, and uncertainty required for international artists simply to obtain the legal right to participate in the U.S. cultural sector.
 
In this way, The O Commons aims not only to share practical resources but also to make visible the hidden bureaucratic infrastructures that shape artistic production, mobility, and cultural participation. By combining mutual aid with creative practice, the project seeks to transform individual immigration experiences into collective knowledge, solidarity, and artistic inquiry.

IMMIGRANT ARTISTS MUTUAL AID
 
A gathering for immigrant artists, cultural workers, students, and allies
Friday, October 23, 2026
 4:00–6:00 PM
 
The People’s Forum
 Josina Muthemba Machel Classroom
 320 W 37th Street, New York, NY 10018
 
The O Commons is a new mutual aid network for immigrant artists and cultural workers. We are coming together to share knowledge, resources, experiences, and creative practices around the realities of making art and building a life in the United States.
 
At this launch gathering, we will:
Share stories, experiences, and immigration resources
 Exchange practical knowledge and support
 Connect with other immigrant artists and cultural workers
 Create short artistic responses to documents, objects, and memories from our immigration journeys
 
Bring a document, object, or memory from your immigration journey if you would like to share it through a short creative intervention—a monologue, performance, visual artwork, installation concept, or another form.
